# Withdraw 25,400,000 ada for Intersect: Governance coordination and technical ...
**BEACN DRep vote: NO**  _(plain-language explanation — precomputed)_

BEACN voted NO because this was a large treasury withdrawal request, and the review found that the cost-benefit and sustainability case was not strong enough for that level of shared-ADA spending.

The action requested 25,400,000 ada for Intersect’s June 2026 to June 2027 operating model, covering governance coordination, technical stewardship, incident response, release coordination, repository management, security initiatives, operations, ecosystem coordination, and critical process management. The proposal broke this into 6,000,000 ada for operations and ecosystem coordination, 18,800,000 ada for technical stewardship and incident response, and 600,000 ada for management of critical processes, with milestone-based drawdowns, reporting, disclosures, and an external assurance process.

Several parts of the proposal were well-supported: the requested amount and budget breakdown were clear, the proposal anchor was available and replayable, prior treasury funding was disclosed, the proposal referenced Intersect budget-process voting and Net Change Limit materials, and it described smart-contract-based treasury management with oversight and dashboard tracking. The strongest YES case was that the proposal clearly substantiated the core funding request and cleared important evidence gates.

The NO came from the remaining risk around a large treasury ask. The review found missing or thin evidence for material claims, including the claim that the ask was reduced from last year while preserving the most critical functions, and claims about past network-upgrade and incident-coordination performance. It also flagged missing treasury sustainability path, cost-benefit clarity, independent assurance in the risk review, and rollback or remedy path. Because treasury withdrawals require elevated scrutiny and the treasury flow signal was in an unsustainable regime, BEACN judged that the cost and precedent risk outweighed the supported benefits.
