BEACN voted NO because this treasury withdrawal did not meet the higher evidence bar for spending shared ADA. The proposal described useful maintenance work for Oura, but the review found major gaps in budget analysis, feasibility, risk, alternatives, failure modes, and community impact.

The action requested 540,750 ada for 12 months of Oura maintenance by TxPipe, submitted by Intersect on behalf of the vendor. The proposal says this covers 525,000 ada for maintenance and enhancement, including a part-time maintainer, dependency updates, Cardano protocol compatibility, performance work, bug fixes, documentation, community support, and AI-friendly integration resources, plus a 15,750 ada Intersect administration fee. It also claims Oura is a Rust-native event pipeline tool for Cardano nodes, with multiple supported data sources and output sinks.

The pinned anchor was available and the proposal had enough detail to review, but the treasury diligence dossier was incomplete. The review specifically found missing or thin evidence for a line-item budget, milestone-gated disbursement, risk mitigations, independent assurance, feasibility, alternatives, failure-mode analysis, and community impact. Because treasury actions consume shared ADA and set precedent, BEACN treated those gaps as decisive and voted NO.
