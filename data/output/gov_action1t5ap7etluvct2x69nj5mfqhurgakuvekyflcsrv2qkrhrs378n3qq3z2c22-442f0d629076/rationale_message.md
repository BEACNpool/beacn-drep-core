# Reforming Treasury Governance
**BEACN DRep vote: ABSTAIN**  _(plain-language explanation — precomputed)_

BEACN voted ABSTAIN because the proposal raises a legitimate treasury-governance topic, but the evidence record did not meet the threshold for a directional vote. The review found a clear public anchor and a coherent governance intent, but several important claims were still asserted rather than independently supported.

The action asked only for an InfoAction: to introduce ideas for reforming Cardano treasury governance, encourage public discussion, and explore processes such as clearer strategic direction, possible constitutional refinements, votes on the NCL and budgets, and a budget structure across eight treasury domains. The proposer argued that current treasury governance is in a poor state, that withdrawal voting has created competition and gridlock, and that Cardano needs more strategic budget governance. The review found some ideas well-supported inside the proposal itself, including that treasury governance should be explicitly addressed, that balanced budgets may be preferable to isolated withdrawals, and that a strategic entity and expert commission could play roles in budget preparation and project evaluation.

The main weakness was evidence quality. The proposal’s anchor document was available and replayable, execution risk was low, and the strongest YES case cleared the evidence gates on the general need to address treasury governance. But key problem statements were not independently evidenced, including the claim that Cardano treasury governance is currently in a poor state with a funding impasse, and the claim that current treasury withdrawal voting has produced hostility, toxicity, and gridlock. The review also noted thin claims-and-evidence status, a flag score that reduced confidence, and missing mitigation or independent assurance evidence in the risk review.

This ABSTAIN is a conservative, evidence-based hold, not active opposition. A directional vote would be unblocked by replayable independent evidence for the major problem claims, clearer support for the proposed institutional design, and stronger mitigation or assurance evidence showing how the suggested treasury-governance process would avoid creating new governance risks.
